## Step 0.5 — Premise audit (detailed)

### M0a. Launch-platform audience-class fit

**Premise tested:** Show HN is the right launch platform for a $40/seat B2B engineering PM tool sold to EMs at 50-500-person SaaS companies.

**Web evidence (live searches in this run):**
- "Show HN: Despatch — Project management for systems thinking" (Apr 2026) is the most recent comparable Show HN attempt — minimal traction visible from search results.
- HN's audience skews IC engineer / OSS / dev-tool / infra. EMs are present but not procurement-active there; mid-market SaaS procurement does not happen via HN discovery.
- Linear — the canonical success in this exact category — explicitly did NOT win via Show HN. They won via Twitter waitlist + hand-picked beta of buddies at Cohere/Runway/Ramp.

**Founder defense:** none provided beyond "founders hang out on HN."

**Verdict: PREMISE FAILS.**

**Implications:**
- Show HN as launch step 1 is wrong-audience-class. HN cohort = IC engineers and OSS hobbyists at 5-50-person early-stage startups, NOT EMs at 200-person Series B SaaS who hold procurement authority for a $40/seat tool.
- Show HN is still useful as a pulse-check / awareness event, but it CANNOT be the primary acquisition vector for the stated ICP.
- M2 (discovery path) will refuse "they see my Show HN" as step 1.

### M0b. Canonical-success comparable

**Premise tested:** plan has anchored against canonical success in this motion class (design-led / opinionated B2B SaaS).

**Web evidence (live searches in this run):**
- Linear (the obvious precedent): founded 2019 by Karri Saarinen (ex-Airbnb head of design), Jori Lallo (ex-Coinbase), Tuomas Artman (ex-Uber). Path to first customers:
  - Built waitlist of ~10,000 via founder Twitter audiences (large pre-existing design/eng audience)
  - Hand-picked first 100-200 users from waitlist via SURVEY identifying most motivated prospects
  - Cofounders' friends at small tech startups (proto-ICP) were the first cohort
  - Early beta companies: Cohere, Runway, Ramp (via founder network, not cold)
  - Bottom-up adoption: one person invites team, team invites other teams (viral inside companies)
  - Series B at $35M led by Accel (later), valuation ~$400M
  - PRICING: $8/seat (less than 1/4 of Threadline's stated $40)
- Other relevant comparables:
  - Superhuman (premium-concierge consumer): Vohra personally onboarded first 200 users; invite-only waitlist; "very disappointed" PMF gate; $30/mo
  - Stripe (developer-first infra): docs as marketing; founder presence; not directly applicable here
  - Notion (productivity SaaS): built waitlist over years; founder built audience first

**Founder defense:** none provided.

**Verdict: PREMISE FAILS.**

**Implications:**
- The single most relevant precedent (Linear) succeeded with FIVE preconditions Threadline lacks: (1) founder with prominent design-Twitter audience, (2) 10K waitlist pre-launch, (3) hand-picked beta cohort from founder network at real ICP companies, (4) bottom-up viral spread driven by polish-as-marketing, (5) $8/seat price competitive with Jira at the IC entry point.
- "Better than Linear" framing is positioning the new product head-on against the playbook winner, at 5x the price, with 0/5 of Linear's preconditions. This is a category error.
- Section 5's playbook is rewritten to follow Linear's pattern (waitlist + concierge beta + bottom-up) NOT Show-HN-first.

### M0c. Anti-feature surface

**Premise tested:** all in-scope features serve the paying ICP.

**Available info:** the design doc is high-level — feature list not supplied. The framing-level claims serve as proxy.

**Anti-positioning identified at the framing level:**
1. **"Linear-style for engineering teams"** — Linear IS the design-led winner for engineering teams. Positioning AS Linear-but-better is not differentiation; it is direct head-on against the entrenched leader without a stated wedge. Anti-positioning candidate.
2. **"Engineering-specific workflows" (unspecified)** — every PM tool claims this. Without a concrete wedge (specific workflow that Linear does badly), the differentiation is vapor. Anti-positioning candidate.
3. **$40/seat with no free tier** — this combination signals "premium enterprise" while the buyer behavior in a 50-200-person scale-up is bottom-up evaluation by an IC who needs to try before convincing their EM/CTO. No free tier blocks the standard bottom-up path; high price suggests top-down sale; founders have NO B2B sales experience. The pricing+packaging combination contradicts the sales motion the founders are capable of.

**Verdict: PARTIAL FAIL.** Can't audit the full feature list, but the FRAMING-LEVEL premises produce 3 contradictions worth flagging.

**Implications for Section 6:** revisit packaging — either (a) add free tier or aggressive trial to enable bottom-up evaluation, OR (b) move price up further into "enterprise per-company" and learn top-down sales fast.

### M0d. Freebie-disqualifier / acquisition-mechanic selection bias

**Premise tested:** $40/seat / no free tier / 14-day trial is the right acquisition mechanic for this ICP.

**Diagnostic (per skill table):**
- "No free tier, 14-day trial" — selects for: people who already trust the tool enough to enter CC OR who are doing a paid evaluation for procurement. Filters out: bottom-up curious ICs who would champion the tool internally.
- 14-day is too short for B2B procurement at 50-500-person companies — typical procurement cycle is 30-90 days; trial expires before the buying committee is even convened.
- For mid-market B2B, the question is not "free or paid trial" — it is "how does the buying committee form, and who advocates internally between when an IC tries it and when the EM signs the PO?" Current plan answers neither.

**Web evidence:**
- Linear's free tier exists (free for personal/small teams), enabling bottom-up evaluation that converts into team plans. This is the default winning playbook for design-led B2B SaaS at this market segment.
- Shortcut is free for ≤10 users — same playbook, bottom-up evaluation.

**Verdict: PREMISE FAILS.**

**Implications for Section 6:** either add a permanent free tier (≤5 seats / single project) to enable bottom-up trial, OR extend trial to 30 days AND build an explicit account-based sales motion the founders do not yet know how to run. Doing neither and shipping the current packaging produces a validation cohort of zero.

### M0e. Dogfood audience-class match (founder vs paying ICP)

**Premise tested:** the audience the product is dogfooded on (2 ex-Google engineers) = the paying ICP (EMs / TLs / ICs at 50-500-person SaaS companies).

**Diagnostic:**
- Ex-Google engineers ship Google's internal process: CL-based code review, internal monorepo norms, internal project tracking that does NOT look like Linear / Jira / Shortcut, internal launch review processes (e.g., LSC, OKR-style planning at scale).
- An EM at a Series B 200-person SaaS company has: small cross-functional squads, GitHub PR review, Linear or Jira incumbent, mixed remote/hybrid, NO LSC, NO Google-scale infra, and pain points around: sprint planning ceremony fatigue, PR-to-ticket linking, async standup, roadmap visibility across squads, integration with on-call tools.
- The Google-engineer instinct (build for power-users with deep process literacy) directly contradicts the scale-up EM instinct (we just need a tool that gets out of our way and lets us not run another half-broken Jira project).

**Per anti-pattern #14:** the correct fix is BOTH pivots:
- (a) **Pivot the TARGET audience in marketing materials and ICP doc:** ICP IS the EM at 50-500-person SaaS — not "anyone who likes Linear." Make this explicit. Update landing page, all positioning to reflect.
- (b) **Expand dogfood subjects:** before launch, do 2 weeks minimum of buyer-aligned dogfood — recruit 3-5 EMs at real 50-500-person SaaS companies (warm intros from ex-Google network) to use Threadline live with their team. Watch usage. Re-tune.

**Refusing the wrong fix:** do NOT defer the EM-at-scale-up audience to "v1.0.5" while keeping Threadline tuned to ex-Google dogfooders. That preserves the mismatch.

**Verdict: PREMISE FAILS. Both pivots required.**

### Pricing-band sub-check

Surfaced here, fully revisited in Section 6:
- Direct competitor band: $8-16/seat (Linear $8, Shortcut $8.50-16, GitHub Projects free with GitHub, Jira $7-15)
- Threadline at $40/seat is 2.5-5x the band
- No documented willingness-to-pay evidence for this premium
- The premium can ONLY be defended by EITHER a sharp wedge (specific workflow worth 5x) OR a different buyer (enterprise/CTO rather than IC/EM)
- Currently defended by neither

## Section 5 — Launch playbook (multi-channel, sequenced)

### 5a. Question the launch gate FIRST

**Stated gate:** "5 paying companies by day 30."

**Question:** is this calendar-driven or PMF-signal-driven? The number (5) is calendar-anchored to a 30-day window, but the bar (paying = some willingness-to-pay signal) is partially PMF-aligned.

**Problem:** for retention-driven B2B SaaS, the right gate is PMF-signal, not "5 logos." 5 paid logos that churn in month 2 is NOT product-market fit. The Vohra/Superhuman pattern (≥40% "very disappointed" if they could no longer use it) or Linear pattern (clear bottom-up viral signal within accounts) is a better gate.

**Proposed replacement gate:** "3-5 paying design-partner companies AND ≥40% of users in those companies answer 'very disappointed' on the Vohra question AND at least 2 of the 5 have unprompted invited a peer EM at another company." This is harder, but it is the right gate.

**Acceptable founder defense to KEEP the calendar gate:** "we're capital-constrained; we need revenue signal in 30 days to decide whether to continue funding this; we'll accept calendar gate with explicit acknowledgment that day-30 traction may not be PMF and will be re-measured at day 60-90." This defense is acceptable. Document it explicitly.

## Section 6 — Pricing & packaging

### 6a. Question the pricing BAND first

**Stated price:** $40/seat/mo, no free tier, 14-day trial.

**Direct competitor band:** $8-16/seat.
**Adjacent-category band (productivity SaaS for knowledge workers):** $10-30/seat (Notion $10-15, Linear $8, ChatGPT Plus $20, Superhuman $30).
**Enterprise PM (Jira Premium, Asana Business+):** $15-25/seat.

**Threadline at $40/seat is OUTSIDE both the direct-competitor band AND the adjacent-category band.** This is band-error, not point-error.

**Required question:** did $40 come from competitor anchoring (no — it is 2.5-5x competitors), from ICP willingness-to-pay evidence (no evidence cited), or from a desired ARPU target (likely — $40 × 5 seats/co × 50 cos = $10K MRR, plausibly the founders' first-revenue goal)?

**Likely failure mode:** $40/seat was reverse-engineered from a target MRR, NOT from any ICP willingness-to-pay signal. This is the most common mistake.

### 6b. The trilemma

Three viable resolutions; pick one and commit:

**Option 1 — Stay at $40/seat, justify the premium with a SHARP wedge.** Requires: a specific workflow Threadline does that Linear/Jira/Shortcut categorically don't (not "better" — different and valuable). Possible angles: AI-driven sprint planning that demonstrably saves 4hr/EM/week; native integration with on-call/SLO/incident-management tools that Linear ignores; multi-squad-roadmap consolidation Linear is bad at. Founder must NAME the wedge and price the premium against it ("Threadline is $40/seat because the average EM saves 4hr/week on sprint planning, valued at $400/wk loaded; ROI is 10x").

**Option 2 — Drop to $12-15/seat with a $0 free tier (≤5 seats / 1 project).** Match the design-led B2B SaaS band. Enable bottom-up evaluation by ICs. Lose top-line per-seat revenue, gain ability to acquire bottom-up like Linear did. Plan-to-revenue path: 100 companies × 10 seats × $12 = $12K MRR — same target as Option 1 with broader top-of-funnel.

**Option 3 — Pivot up to enterprise per-company pricing ($1-3K/mo flat per company up to N seats).** Target larger 200-500-person companies with explicit AE-style outbound. Requires the founders to learn B2B sales motion they don't currently have. Slowest start, highest ceiling. Probably wrong for these founders' current capability.

**Recommendation:** Option 2 is the highest-probability win given founder constraints. Option 1 is viable if (and only if) a sharp wedge exists that the founders can demonstrate. Option 3 is the wrong fit for a 2-person no-sales-experience team.

### 6c. Packaging coverage

- **Free tier:** add one. ≤5 seats, 1 project, all features. Enables bottom-up evaluation.
- **Starter:** $12-15/seat. Unlimited seats, all features, community support.
- **Business:** $20-25/seat. SSO, admin controls, analytics, priority support.
- **Enterprise:** custom. SAML, audit logs, dedicated CSM. Only sell this after first 20 customers exist.
- **Trial:** if keeping paid-only, extend to 30 days (matches mid-market procurement cycle). 14 days is too short. Better: replace trial with free-tier-converts-to-paid.
- **Annual discount:** 20% off (industry standard for mid-market SaaS). Annual billing materially improves retention — RevenueCat data and standard SaaS playbook.
- **Per-seat model:** keep. Per-company is wrong for the 50-500-person band (too much variance). Per-usage is wrong for PM tools (doesn't map to value).

### 6d. M0d follow-through (acquisition-mechanic selection bias)

Per M0d: 14-day-trial-with-CC selects for evaluators willing to commit, but FILTERS OUT the bottom-up IC champion who is the actual viral vector for design-led B2B SaaS in this band. Adding the free tier resolves the selection bias.

- Paid ads (every new customer requires new spend)

## Outside voice — independent marketing critique

Simulated independent critique (the skill prescribes a Claude subagent or Codex CLI; in this regression test, simulating in-line):

### 1. Single biggest marketing risk the inside review missed

**"Threadline" the name is generic and undifferentiated.** Searches for "threadline" return unrelated results, complicating SEO. The name doesn't signal what the product does or who it's for. Other PM tools have names that either signal speed (Linear, Shortcut) or process (Jira from Gojira/Godzilla, Plane). Threadline reads like a thread management tool or a fashion brand. The inside review focused on positioning, pricing, channels — but the name itself is a marketing liability. Consider: rename, OR own a specific noun-pair like "Threadline for Engineering Teams" everywhere to disambiguate.

### 2. Channel mentioned that's weaker than positioned

**LinkedIn for the founders is positioned as their strongest channel — but it might be weaker than estimated.** "Ex-Google engineers with 500-1500 LinkedIn contacts" assumes the contacts are EMs/Directors at scale-up SaaS — but a typical ex-Google IC engineer's LinkedIn network is heavily other-Google + other-big-tech ICs, NOT EMs at 200-person SaaS. If <15% are ICP, downgrade LinkedIn from #2 to #4 and elevate cold outbound to compensate (with the corresponding sales-motion learning curve).

**The "200 X followers + 50-person email list" claim is undefended on ICP alignment.** Inside review noted this but didn't recommend the founders explicitly classify the 200 + 50 in week 1. Recommendation: founders manually segment their 250 combined contacts by (a) holds engineering management role, (b) at a 50-500-person SaaS company, (c) has procurement authority for tools >$200/mo. Likely outcome: ~5-15 truly ICP-aligned contacts. That's the actual starting audience. Plan accordingly.

### 4. Provocative reframe

**Reframe: Threadline shouldn't compete with Linear at all. It should compete with the COMPOSITE STACK that scale-up engineering teams currently glue together (Jira + Slack + Notion + PagerDuty + Linear-for-design-team).** The wedge isn't "better than Linear" — it's "the integrated workflow layer for the moment when one PM tool isn't enough." This reframe (a) escapes the head-on-Linear positioning, (b) justifies a premium price (replacing 3 tools at $5-15/seat each = $15-45/seat ceiling defensible), (c) targets the exact pain Priya has in week 1 of Q3 (her Jira + Slack + Notion is fragmenting her team's context). Founders should test this reframe in week-1 design-partner interviews: ask "if Threadline could replace 3 of your current tools, which 3 would have the highest ROI?" — the answer reveals the actual wedge.
