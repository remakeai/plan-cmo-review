## Primary persona — Sarah Chen (corporate / structural-moat segment)

### Who she is
- 38, female, In-house Counsel at a mid-size biotech (Boston).
- 7 years in-house after 5 years at an AmLaw 100 firm.
- Outlook 365 corporate email.
- IT policy: OAuth integrations to email are forbidden (a documented written policy; not just convention). Shortwave, Notion Mail, Superhuman AI are structurally unavailable to her.
- Household income comfortable enough that $9-30/mo professional tools are unconscious spend; she does not price-shop in this category.

### What she pays for currently (adjacent categories)
- ChatGPT Plus — $30/mo (her own credit card)
- Morning Brew premium — $14/mo
- WSJ Digital — $99/yr
- Bloomberg subscription — $20/mo personal portion
- Audible — $15/mo
- Notion Personal Pro — $10/mo
- Intermittent trials of productivity tools ($10-15/mo, ~3/year)
- **Cumulative monthly spend on professional/information tools: $80-110/mo personal + employer-paid tools (Westlaw, Bloomberg Law Pro, etc.)**

### Where she spends attention (5+ places)
1. **LinkedIn** — active poster ~weekly, comments on industry posts, ~600 connections in legal/biotech
2. **Legal-Twitter** — lurker, reads but doesn't post; follows ~20 legal-tech and biotech-counsel accounts
3. **Above the Law morning briefing** — daily, reads first 3 stories most days
4. **Bloomberg Law Daily** — daily, reads selectively
5. **Stat News Health Tech** — daily, weeks she has time
6. **Lawyerist podcast** — commute listening, 2-3 episodes/week
7. **"Stress Test" podcast** — commute, weekly
8. **Private legal-tech Slack** (~400 members) — lurker, trusts 4-5 power posters who recommend tools
9. **JD Supra Health Law Daily** — daily skim
10. **Axios Pro Codebook** — daily, the most-opened of her newsletter set
11. **Politico Pulse** — daily skim
12. **Morning Brew** — daily skim, weekend reads more carefully

### Newsletter inbox state
- ~7 daily newsletters arriving in work Outlook
- Opens ~3 fully
- Deletes ~4 unread
- Mild guilt about the delete pattern; "missing stories that mattered"
- Has tried Newsletter folders, filter rules — abandoned each within 2 weeks

### What she complains about (in adjacent categories)
- "Tools I bought never get used because I forget to open another app" → tldrof comes to her inbox, doesn't need another app
- "I can't OAuth my work email into anything — IT blocks it" → tldrof's forwarding architecture is dispositive
- "AI summaries lose the analytical nuance" → wants 2-3 sentence summaries that read like an analytical lede, not bullet points
- "I don't have time to evaluate 5 productivity tools" → tldrof needs to land via trusted recommendation, not via her own research
- "Annual billing locks me in; I prefer monthly" — but in practice retains on annual

### Objections she will raise (and the answer)

1. **"Is this safe for client-confidential emails?"** — TRUST OBJECTION (highest stakes). Answer: forwarding-only architecture (no inbox OAuth), explicit "we never store beyond N days," dedicated privacy/security page she can forward to her IT director, optionally SOC 2 promise on roadmap.
2. **"$9 (or $12) feels arbitrary."** — PRICE BAND OBJECTION. Answer: this is at or below her Bloomberg subscription portion ($20/mo). At the price band of Morning Brew premium ($14/mo). The price IS the message — premium-but-not-luxury.
3. **"Does it work with my existing newsletters or do I have to resubscribe?"** — SETUP-FRICTION OBJECTION. Answer: auto-forward setup helper (v1.0 per pass 4) — generates a Gmail/Outlook filter URL or rule export. Setup ~2 minutes per email client, no resubscribe required. THIS IS THE KILLER FEATURE for Sarah's segment.
4. **"Will I miss the actual interesting stories?"** — QUALITY OBJECTION. Answer: time-saved math is the proxy. Need to show ONE first-digest that includes a story she'd have read in full, and her validation that the summary preserved the analytical content.

### Reasons she will convert

1. **Trusted-source recommendation.** A respected legal-tech-Slack power poster says "stopped reading newsletters in my inbox, get one digest, ~$9, doesn't touch your inbox." Sarah signs up that day.
2. **First-digest validation.** She forwards her morning newsletter batch, sees the first digest in 60 seconds, the "30 minutes saved today" math feels accurate, the summaries don't sound like ChatGPT slop.
3. **Trial-end reflexive math.** Day 6 reminder: $9/mo is less than what she was paying in time. Auto-charge fires day 7. She doesn't notice the charge; she notices the digest in her inbox.

### What would make her CHURN

1. Two consecutive bad digests where she opens the underlying newsletter to verify and finds the summary missed the lede
2. A privacy incident (a story about email-forwarding tools getting breached)
3. Her IT department says no (rare given the forwarding architecture, but possible)
4. Forgetting she subscribed and noticing the $9 charge on her statement at month 4

### Why he's NOT DEFERRED (per M0e v0.3 fix)

- He's a real, live, paying market. We don't ship the product calibrated to Sarah and ignore him.
- HN launch is real distribution. We design for him in the HN launch copy AND on the secondary "for AI/dev professionals" landing-page section.
- The dogfood-pivot work in Section 4 EXPANDS dogfood subjects to include Sarah-cohort newsletters — it does NOT REPLACE Alex-cohort newsletters.

- Free competitor (OSS clone) emerges; Alex switches because he can self-host
