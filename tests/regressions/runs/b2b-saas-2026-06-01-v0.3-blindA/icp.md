# ICP — Threadline

**Plan-cmo-review run:** v0.3-blindA, 2026-06-02

## Named persona (composite)

**Maya Chen, 34, Director of Engineering at "Brightwave"** (fictional composite — represents a real cohort).

- **Company:** 180-person Series B SaaS, NYC HQ + 30% remote. Post-Series-B ($35M raise, late 2024). Vertical: B2B horizontal productivity. ~$15M ARR.
- **Team:** 4 squad leads reporting to her, 28 IC engineers total. Two squads on platform infra, two on product features.
- **Tenure:** 7 years in engineering, 2.5 years as manager, 8 months at Director level. Promoted internally after her predecessor left.
- **Stack:** Linear (one squad piloted ~6 months ago, hasn't rolled out company-wide), Jira (rest of company, inherited from 2022), GitHub Enterprise, PagerDuty, Datadog, Notion (docs), Slack, Linear → Slack notifications integration.
- **Budget authority:** up to $5K/month tooling without VP Eng sign-off; up to $25K/month with sign-off.

## Five+ specific attributes

1. **Pain (specific):** weekly planning meetings run 90 minutes when she budgeted 60 because cross-squad dependencies surface only verbally. Quarterly OKR rollups require her to manually export Linear + Jira, paste into Google Sheets, reconcile dupes, build a deck. ~6 hours per quarter she resents.
2. **Buying psychology:** burned once by buying a tool (in 2024) the team didn't adopt. Now over-rotates to "will my team actually use this?" Trial-converts only when at least 1 squad lead champions internally.
3. **Status anxieties:** worries her CTO thinks she's slow on cross-squad visibility. Looks for tools that produce executive-ready artifacts (rollups, dashboards) WITHOUT having to build them herself.
4. **Information diet:** Lenny's Newsletter (paid), Pragmatic Engineer (paid), Software Engineering Daily, Engineering Enablement podcast, LeadDev YouTube. Lurks Rands Slack. Skims her CTO's LinkedIn shares.
5. **FAANG sensitivity:** has read enough Pragmatic Engineer to know FAANG-flavored advice often doesn't apply at 180 people. Reads ex-Google credentials with mild skepticism unless paired with explicit "and here's why this scales down."
6. **Time:** 11-hour days. Buys time-saving tools quickly when the case is clear; ignores cold outreach that doesn't reference a specific pain she has.

## Specific objections she would raise to Threadline

1. "We just rolled out Linear last quarter. Why would I switch?" → Threadline must answer: "You don't switch. We complement Linear by giving you the cross-squad layer Linear doesn't have." (See outside-voice reframe in main plan.)
2. "$40/seat is more than Linear. I'd have to justify the delta to my VP." → Threadline must answer: this is the pricing-band question; pick (A) cheaper than Linear, or (B) explicitly mission-critical positioning.
3. "Will my squad leads actually use it?" → Threadline must answer: design-partner case studies showing usage by squad leads, not just EMs.
4. "Are you going to be around in 2 years?" → Threadline must answer: ex-Google credibility helps partially; named investors, paid design partners, public traction help more.
5. "I'm already drowning. I can't pilot a new tool right now." → Threadline must answer: concierge onboarding, founders set up cross-squad view with her real data on a 30-minute call.

## Specific reasons she would convert

1. A peer EM at a comparable company tells her at LeadDev that Threadline solved their cross-squad meeting problem.
2. Her own squad lead pilots Threadline on a side project, shows her the cross-squad dependency view, says "this would save us 30 minutes a week per squad."
3. Threadline produces an OKR rollup deck automatically, in a quality her CTO would accept.
4. Pricing fits cleanly in her $5K/month discretionary budget for a 4-squad pilot (4 × ~7 seats × $40 = $1120/month, comfortably under).
5. Founder personally onboards her — implicit signal of seriousness + premium service positioning.

## Dogfood-correction note (from M0e)

The current dogfood subject (the two ex-Google founders themselves) does NOT represent Maya. Google's engineering culture (massive team scale, fixed tooling stack, internal infrastructure abstractions) differs sharply from a 180-person Series B SaaS. **Mandate:** recruit 3-5 EMs matching this ICP as paid design partners ($1-3K/yr commitment) BEFORE further product build. Dogfood the product on THEIR workflows, not on the founders' imagined-Google workflows.
